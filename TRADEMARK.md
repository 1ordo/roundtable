# The name and the mark

The code is under the GNU AGPL v3, and that licence is deliberately
permissive about what you may do with the code. It says nothing about names,
because copyright licences never do. This file covers the gap.

**"Roundtable", the Roundtable wordmark, and the logo in `branding/` are not
licensed to you.** They are reserved by the copyright holder.

## What this means in practice

You may, without asking:

- Run Roundtable, for yourself or for other people, free or paid.
- Modify it, fork it, and publish your fork under the AGPL.
- Say truthfully that your project is "based on Roundtable", "a fork of
  Roundtable", or "compatible with Roundtable".
- Use the name in comparisons, documentation, articles and talks.
- Keep the name and branding intact on an unmodified copy you are simply
  redistributing or hosting.

Please do not, without written permission:

- Publish a modified version under the name Roundtable, or under a name close
  enough to be mistaken for it.
- Use the logo or wordmark as the identity of your own product or service.
- Imply that your fork, service or company is the official Roundtable, or is
  endorsed by or affiliated with it.
- Register the name, the mark, or a confusingly similar one, anywhere.

## Why this exists rather than a more restrictive code licence

The point is not to stop anyone using the software. Companies are welcome to
run it, and the AGPL already requires that anyone offering a modified version
over a network publishes their changes, which is the protection that actually
matters.

The point is that a name is the one thing a licence cannot protect and a fork
cannot legitimately take. Somebody who improves Roundtable and ships it as
their own fork is doing exactly what open source is for. Somebody who ships
it unchanged as their own product, under this name, is not.

If you want to do something this file does not allow, ask. The answer is
usually yes.
