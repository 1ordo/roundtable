Roundtable

Browser extension

Privacy policy

Last updated: 10 August 2026

The extension sends what you watch to your own Roundtable instance, at the address you typed into its options page. It does not send anything to the author of Roundtable, or to anyone else, because there is nowhere else for it to go. No analytics, no tracking, no advertising, no third-party service of any kind.

What it collects

Only on the streaming sites listed in its permissions, and only while you are on them:

  • The page address, to recognise which service you are on and whether you are on a playback page.
  • The title the page reports, from the document title or the browser's media-session metadata, plus the season and episode number when the page states them.
  • How far through you are, as a fraction, read from the page's video element.
  • Your watchlist, when you press the import button in the extension popup: the titles listed on that service's own list page.

It does not read anything else on those pages. It does not touch any other site. It has no access to your streaming account, your password, your payment details, or your viewing history on the service itself.

Where it goes

To the Roundtable instance URL you configured, authenticated with a personal API token you generated there. Nowhere else.

Roundtable is self-hosted software. That instance is a server you or someone you know runs. Whoever operates it controls that data, and the extension's author has no access to it and no way to obtain it.

What is stored in the browser

  • The instance address and your API token, in extension storage.
  • A short queue of events not yet delivered, so nothing is lost when the network drops or the instance is briefly unreachable. It is emptied as soon as delivery succeeds.

Clearing the extension's storage, or removing the extension, removes both.

What it never does

  • Send anything to the extension author or any third party.
  • Run remote code. Everything the extension executes ships in the package.
  • Read pages other than the streaming services it declares.
  • Sell, share or otherwise pass on your information. There is nobody to pass it to.

Removing your data

The data lives on your Roundtable instance. Delete individual entries from your history in the app, or export everything from Settings and then ask the operator to remove your account. Uninstalling the extension stops anything further being sent.

Permissions, and why

Permission Why
storage Remembering your instance address, token, and the pending queue
tabs Knowing which service page is open, and opening a list page when you press import
notifications Telling you when something was logged, or could not be matched
Host access to the streaming sites Reading the title and playback position on pages you are already watching

Host access is requested for the specific services listed in the manifest and for nothing else.
