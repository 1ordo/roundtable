# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to help. Argue about the code,
not the person writing it.

## What is expected

- Take the most charitable reading of what somebody wrote. Most of the
  people here are not writing in their first language, and tone travels
  badly in a pull request.
- Criticise the work, plainly and specifically. "This leaks a friends-only
  post to a stranger" is useful. "This is sloppy" is not.
- Say when you are unsure. Confidence you do not have wastes everyone's time.
- Let people leave. Nobody here owes anybody a patch, a review, or a reply
  by any particular date, including the maintainer.

## What is not

- Harassment, whether public or in direct messages.
- Comments that go after somebody's identity rather than their argument:
  their race, nationality, religion, gender, sexuality, disability, age or
  how they speak.
- Sexual attention or imagery anywhere in the project.
- Publishing anyone's private information.
- Deliberately wasting people's time: bad-faith argument, repeated derailing,
  or reopening a settled decision to relitigate it.

This applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, code
comments and anywhere else the project happens.

## If something goes wrong

Report it to **mohemed4442003@gmail.com**. Reports are read by the
maintainer and are not shared further without your agreement.

Say what happened and where. You do not need to prove anything or write it
up formally; a link and a sentence is enough. Reporting something in good
faith will never be held against you, whatever the outcome.

Responses run from a private word, through edited or removed content, to a
temporary or permanent ban. Which one depends on severity and on whether it
has happened before. The maintainer will say what was decided and why, at
least to the person who reported it.

## Scope

This project. If something happens elsewhere and it makes people here unsafe
around a participant, it is in scope too.
